OUR PAST TODAY

Data Project by Jamie Zheng

StuyTown's Demographic History

Understanding the society we live in from its foundational roots; history is a series of events that build on top of one another, each layer peeled back provides context for our current reality

1940 1960 1980 2000 2026

My idea for this project after personal observation of the residents of Stuyvesant Town-Peter Cooper Village (or commonly known as StuyTown) over the course of two weeks was to make paper Russian nesting dolls that show the demographic make-up of StuyTown since its start in the 1940’s up until current day. Focusing on the historical aspect of the residential area located in Manhattan's East Village, this project seeks to show the change in people and trends over a period of time, as well as provide a further understanding of the society we live by re-discovering what has been hidden with the passing of the decades.

This project utilizes not only personal observations that were collected by me, but also outside datasets and articles that hold information of Stuytown's demographics from when it was first built, around 1945, to today, in 2026, where it now currently resides roughly 25,000 to 30,000 people.

It is a project that holds a lot of rich history and information. I have barely begun to scratch the surface of the context of it all; there is so much more that could be said about the bigger picture that these pieces of information hold within them, for not only StuyTown, but living in New York City as a whole; the idea of creating a more suburban residential space within a very fast paced urban city, the economic changes that have occurred and what it means for the residents, and simply just overall, cities as a system and how they operate to either help or hurt the individuals within that system.

I utilized the nature of Russian Nesting dolls to show how history is layers built on top of one another, and with each new layer that is uncovered, something from its past is discovered. It is also a good visualization of evolution, making the data seem more engaging and comprehensible. The dolls were made using traditional origami folding techniques, I felt this would make the un-nesting process more intentional and interesting for those who would handle the doll.

As such, I recognize that the scope of what this project covers may not be entirely complete and will have pieces of it omitted. It is more complex than it initially looks, there are so many things that are interwoven and connected with one another, such is the nature of history and society, it would have been very difficult for me to cover everything. With that in mind, I will only be covering a small part of that complex history by examining the residents of StuyTown and how the demographic has evolved to what it is today. I do encourage viewers of this project to do some research of their own to learn more about the larger history of StuyTown as it is very fascinating, and perhaps that will lead to an ignited curiosity about their own current place of living, prompting even more research. As with any project, the hope is to inform and inspire, and thus, I hope this project will be able to do just that.

These are some of the resources that I used to get my information from and I think provide very interesting insight into the topic are: The New York Times, JSTOR, Vital City NYC, Gotham Gazette, Observer, and YouTube, to name a few.
